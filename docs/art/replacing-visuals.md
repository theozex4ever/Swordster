# Replacing the player and sword art

How to swap the placeholder pictures for real art. You do not edit any gameplay or script file: you add PNGs,
edit one settings file per kind of picture, and look at the result. Pictures never change how the game plays: how
far a sword reaches, what it hits and when, how fast anything moves are all decided elsewhere, so a picture of any
size is safe.

The `textured` look that ships in the repository (`godot/art/placeholder/`) is a set of placeholder drawings
(generated by `tools/godot/gen_placeholder_art.gd`, original, not final art). Use it as a worked example.

## What you provide

| Picture | Settings file | What it needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Player | `godot/art/placeholder/actor_settings.tres` | one sheet of frames, a ground shadow |
| Spirit swords | `godot/art/placeholder/sword_settings.tres` | one sheet per sword type (Fang, Warden, Pike, Colossus), a soft glow, a ground shadow |

Copy the folder (for example to `godot/art/mine/`) so the placeholders stay as a reference, put your PNGs there and
point the copied settings at them.

## 1. Draw frames in a row

A sheet is one PNG with equal-size frames side by side, left to right. Frames of one sheet share one size; sheets of
different swords may have different sizes. For the placeholders: the player is 5 frames of 64x80 (stand, stand,
walk, walk, fallen) and each sword is 4 frames (idle, idle glint, windup, strike).

- **Swords** are drawn pale (white and grey): the game tints the blade with the sword's element (fire, ice, poison).
  Colour baked into the blade shows through the tint.
- **Rarity** is shown by the game: a Rare or Mythic blade is drawn bigger and rings with a glow in the rarity's
  colour (a Common sword's glow takes the element's colour). Keep a clear silhouette so the glow reads.
- Leave a few transparent pixels around the art; edges are filtered.

## 2. Set size, pivot, orientation (per sheet)

Each sheet has a block with these fields (select the settings file in Godot's inspector, or edit the text):

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `texture` | your PNG |
| `frame_count` | how many frames the PNG holds |
| `pivot` | the point of ONE frame, in pixels from its top-left, that sits on the thing's position. For the player: the middle of the body. For a sword: the guard, the place the blade turns about |
| `visual_scale` | how much bigger or smaller than the file the art is drawn (0.5 draws it half size) |
| `orientation_degrees` | the way your art points in the file, measured clockwise from right: art that points **up** is 90, **down** is -90, **right** is 0, **left** is 180 |

The game turns your picture to the sword's heading (or the player's facing) plus `orientation_degrees`, so a blade
drawn pointing up still swings correctly.

## 3. Map animations to what is happening

A clip is a short flip-book: `frames` (which frame numbers, in order), `fps` (frames per second) and `loop`.

- **Player**: `idle_clip` (standing), `moving_clip` (walking), `dead_clip` (fallen). While the player cannot be
  hurt it blinks between full opacity and `invulnerable_alpha`.
- **Swords**: `idle_clip`, `windup_clip` (about to strike), `strike_clip` (also used while stuck in an enemy),
  `recover_clip`, `ultimate_clip`. `lift_idle` and `lift_attack` set how high the sword floats above its shadow
  at rest and while attacking.

Clips are cosmetic. A clip that is longer or shorter than the real attack does not change the attack: the picture
simply keeps showing its last frame (or loops). Make `windup_clip` read as "about to strike", `strike_clip` as
"striking", so players can read the fight.

## 4. Point the game at your art

Make two scene copies of `godot/scenes/textured_actor.tscn` and `textured_sword.tscn` (or edit them) so their
`settings` field uses your settings files. A new look is selected by name in
`godot/scripts/presentation/visual_sets.gd` (add one entry with your scenes). The enemies keep their drawn look in the
`textured` set; they use the same replaceable view contract and can follow the same way.

## 5. Look at it

Run the game with your look selected:

- Windows build: `Swordster.exe -- --visuals=textured`
- Browser build: `index.html?visuals=textured`
- Editor: run the project with `--visuals=textured` as a user argument.

Add `--encounter=drops` (or `?encounter=drops`) to get armed foes carrying every sword type, rarity and element
to look at. Press **Tab** to show attack ranges. Check:

1. **Alignment**: the player's body sits on the centre of the range circle; each blade turns about its guard and
   points where the sword attacks; the shadow stays on the ground.
2. **Phases**: windup, strike and ultimate look different from idle; a sword that is hit-stopped or stuck still reads.
3. **Element and rarity**: three elements are told apart at a glance; Common, Rare and Mythic are told apart by
   the glow and size.
4. **Hover**: swords lying on the ground hover point-down; a Colossus rises as it winds up and its shadow shrinks.

## 6. Prove the fight did not change

`./build-godot.sh --checks-only` replays the same scripted fights with the default and with the textured look, with
art of a very different size, pivot, orientation and animation speed, and requires every movement, hit, damage,
cooldown and target to be identical tick for tick. If it passes, your art is only art.
